02. how_i_work
Priority

Coverage is not even: I prioritize by risk, user impact, and business criticality. Lower-risk scenarios remain in exploratory or deferred testing.

Reporting

A well-written defect includes precise reproduction steps, expected vs actual results, evidence, and the environment. That discipline reduces bug reproduction time and avoids needless back-and-forth with development.

Automation

I automate stable, high-value flows — regression and critical business cases. UI that changes often or requires human judgment stays in manual exploratory testing.

AI

I use AI tools to speed up case design, evidence generation, and documentation. The decision of what to test and why remains a professional judgment, not something to delegate.

Process

I do not just execute the QA process that is handed to me: in the projects I lead, I’m the reference point between QA, development, and product for deciding when something is ready for release. I established the bug-report standard and the test-plan/test-suite documentation the team uses so onboarding does not depend on me being available.

Test strategy

Approach when launching a new product

Risk-based Test planning
First week
I map the most critical business flows together with product — I do not start writing cases until I understand what is most expensive to fail.
Prioritization
I prioritize by risk (impact × probability), not by development order: the critical path is tested first even when another feature appears almost ready.
Coverage
I decide what stays in regression, what stays in manual exploratory testing, and what is intentionally not covered because the risk does not justify it — coverage is not evenly distributed by design.
“Ready for release”
I align with dev and product on the criteria that must be satisfied (minimum coverage, open bugs by severity) before green-lighting release — this is not a QA-only decision.
Accessibility audit

WCAG 2.1 — finding and remediation

WCAG 2.1 AA Manual audit
Criterion
1.4.11 Non-text Contrast (AA)
Finding
The focus state of checkout inputs had a contrast ratio of ~1.8:1 against the background — nearly invisible for keyboard users and low-vision users.
Impact
Users navigating by keyboard or using a screen reader with zoom cannot tell which field is active; this blocks the payment flow and is not a cosmetic issue.
Remediation
Use a solid 2px outline with contrast ≥3:1 plus a secondary visual indicator on the field background instead of relying only on color.
Root cause analysis

Intermittent checkout bug

RCA SQL Logs
Symptom
The subscription status stayed “Active” for up to 8s after payment, and sometimes would not appear until refresh.
Investigation
Logs showed the payment webhook arrived before the SQL subscription insert finished committing — a race condition between the asynchronous event and the database write.
Root cause
The endpoint returned 201 before the commit was confirmed; the frontend poller sometimes read the record just before it existed.
Verification
Reproduced by forcing timing in a local environment; fix validated by moving the response after the commit and adding retry with backoff in the polling flow.
Bug report

Ticket ready for development, without back-and-forth

Jira Bug report
Summary
The “Confirm payment” button remains disabled if the quantity in the cart is edited during checkout.
Steps to reproduce
  1. Add a product to the cart and proceed to checkout.
  2. Edit the quantity from the item input.
  3. Without reloading the page, click “Confirm payment”.
Expected vs. actual
Expected: the total recalculates and the button allows confirmation. Actual: the button remains disabled indefinitely and only re-enables after a page refresh.
Environment · Severity
Chrome 126, Staging, test account with monthly plan · High — blocks checkout whenever quantity is edited.

Data integrity

Detection of orphaned records

SQL Data integrity 
-- Active subscriptions that are missing a mobile cache reflection
SELECT p.subscription_id, p.user_id, p.updated_at
FROM payments.subscriptions p
LEFT JOIN mobile.subscription_cache m
  ON m.subscription_id = p.subscription_id
WHERE p.status = 'active'
  AND (m.subscription_id IS NULL
       OR m.synced_at < p.updated_at - INTERVAL '15 minutes');

Runs during regression across web and mobile platforms to catch desynchronization before users report it.
